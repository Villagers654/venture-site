Venture 0.2.0.
Less stutter. Your controls. Your room.

Our biggest update since launch. Games stutter far less, every controller gets a proper bindings page, you can set resolution and frame rate per game, and mixed reality games show your real room on many more setups.

Less stutter.

A Quest game's code is built for ARM processors and its shaders for a phone GPU. Venture translates both as you play. Every time a game reached something new, that work showed up as a hitch, and it happened again on every launch. 0.2.0 keeps that work, and gets ahead of it.

  1. Translated code is saved.

    It's compressed and stored on disk, so the next launch loads it instead of translating again. When a game updates, only what changed is redone.

  2. Code is translated ahead.

    Spare CPU threads translate the code a game is likely to reach soon, at low priority, out of the game's way.

  3. Shaders are compiled ahead.

    Venture records the pipelines each game builds and compiles them in the background on the next launch, before the game asks.

Controls, done properly.

The new Controls page shows your actual controller, drawn from the 3D model already on your PC, with a line from every button to what it does in Quest games.

  • Starts on your controller.

    It opens on the controller you have connected. Search for any other.

  • Any button, anywhere.

    Every Quest button can go on a single button, a held button, or two buttons pressed together.

  • A menu button that just works.

    Automatic picks a free button your runtime doesn't reserve, and shows you which one.

  • Tuned for every common controller.

    Index, Vive, Touch, PSVR2 Sense, Steam Frame, Windows Mixed Reality, Pico 4, Focus 3 and more. Nothing shared by default, nothing on a button SteamVR keeps for itself.

  • Bindings per game.

    Any game can have its own controls. Turn on custom controls on its page, and Venture tells you when two actions share a button.

Resolution and frame rate, your way.

Games run at the size and refresh rate your VR runtime picks, unless you say otherwise. Now you can, globally or per game.

Mixed reality on more setups.

Mixed reality games now show your room on any setup that can provide it. Venture doesn't look for specific headsets or apps: it asks your runtime what it can do and uses the best option.

  • Quest Link and Air Link

    With Meta's passthrough over Link turned on.

  • SteamVR headsets with a camera

    Such as Index and Vive Pro, directly or through Rectus's OpenXR SteamVR Passthrough layer.

  • Steam Frame

    With McFredward's steam-link-native-passthrough. McFredward also contributed Venture's first passthrough support.

  • Color-key tools

    Virtual Desktop, ALVR and steam-frame-chroma-key: choose Color key in Settings and match the color in your tool.

  • Monado and WiVRn on Linux

    When the driver for your headset supports passthrough.

  • On by default.

    If your setup can't show your room, games simply play in VR. Setup guides for each option are on the Mixed Reality wiki page.

Fixes.

  • Beat Saber

    No longer crashes when SteamVR's resolution is very high. GLES games also use less video memory: Beat Saber about 560 MB less at high resolution.

  • Open Brush

    Open Brush, and other apps whose Java code was optimized in a way PC Java rejects, now start.

  • And many more

    Lots of smaller fixes from your reports. Thank you for sending them.
